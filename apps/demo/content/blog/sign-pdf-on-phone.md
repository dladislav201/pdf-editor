Someone sends you a PDF to sign and you’re away from your computer. You don’t need to print it, and you don’t need an app: your phone’s browser is enough.

## Signing on an iPhone

1. Save the PDF to **Files** (in Mail, touch and hold the attachment, then **Save to Files**).
2. Open the **Sign a PDF** page in Safari and tap **Choose a PDF**, then pick the file.
3. The signature window opens. Choose how to sign:
   - **Draw**: sign with your finger in the box.
   - **Type**: type your name and pick a handwriting style.
   - **Upload**: use a photo of your signature on white paper.
4. Tap **Place signature**, then tap where it goes. Drag it into position and pull the corner to resize it.
5. Tap **Download**. Safari saves the signed PDF to **Files › Downloads**, ready to attach to a reply.

## Signing on Android

The steps are the same in Chrome. Pick the PDF from your downloads or from Google Drive, sign, place and download. The signed file lands in **Downloads**.

## Tips for a neat signature

- **Turn the phone sideways** when drawing: the box gets wider and your signature less cramped.
- **Draw it larger than you need.** You can scale it down on the page, and it stays smooth.
- **Reuse it.** Your last signatures are remembered in your browser, so next time it’s one tap. They’re stored only on your phone, never uploaded.

## Is this a legal signature?

What you add is a picture of your signature on the page, the same as signing a printout and scanning it. That’s what most everyday paperwork asks for: rental forms, school letters, delivery notes, many contracts.

Some documents require a certificate-based digital signature (a qualified electronic signature). That’s a different, cryptographic thing, and it isn’t supported here yet. If the sender asks for one specifically, use the tool they recommend.
