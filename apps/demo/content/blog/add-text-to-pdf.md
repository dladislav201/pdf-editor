A school sends a permission slip as a PDF, or an office emails an application form. You open it, click on the line where your name goes, and nothing happens. The form looks fillable, but it’s really a picture of a printed page, with no fields in it.

You don’t have to print it, fill it in by hand and scan it back. You can type on it.

## Forms with fields

Try clicking first. If the PDF has real fields, they’re found when it opens, and you just click one and type. **Tab** jumps to the next field, and checkboxes tick with a click. There’s a [separate guide for those](/blog/fill-pdf-form-without-acrobat).

## Forms without fields: type with Note

1. Open the PDF and pick **Note** in the tools.
2. Click where your text goes, for example just above the line for your name.
3. Type your answer.
4. Click the next place and type again: name, date, address, each one where it belongs.

To move a note, pick **Select** and drag it. To change it, double-click it. To remove one, select it and delete it.

## Tick the boxes

Use **Note** with an “X” or a “✓” on each box, or **Draw** to mark it by hand.

## Sign at the bottom

Most forms end with a signature. Click **Sign**, draw or type your signature, then place it on the line. It’s remembered in your browser for the next form.

## Save and send

Click **Download**. Your notes are saved as standard PDF text annotations, so they show up in Acrobat, Preview, Chrome and on phones, and the person who receives the form can still read it if they print it.

## Private by design

Forms ask for exactly the things you shouldn’t upload to a random website: your address, date of birth, ID numbers. Here the form is filled in your browser and never leaves your device.
