Invoices, delivery notes and contracts in many countries still need the company stamp next to the signature. Printing, stamping and scanning every document takes a while. With a photo of the stamp you do it once and reuse it.

## 1. Take the photo

Stamp a clean sheet of white paper and photograph it with your phone:

- **Daylight or a bright lamp**, no flash: flash leaves a glare spot.
- **Hold the phone flat over the paper**, so the round stamp stays round.
- **Fill the frame** with the stamp. More pixels mean sharper edges.

A shadow across the paper is fine. It’s removed with the paper.

## 2. Turn it into a transparent stamp

1. Open the PDF and click **Sign**.
2. Choose **Photo**, then **Choose a photo**, and pick the picture.
3. The paper turns transparent, and so do its shadows and the grey of a phone photo. What’s left is the ink.
4. If specks of paper remain, move **Clean up the background** further. If thin lines of the stamp start to fade, move it back.
5. Leave the ink colour on **As in the photo** so a blue or violet stamp stays blue or violet. Black and Blue recolour it, which suits a signature better than a stamp.

## 3. Place it

Click **Place signature**, then click where the stamp goes. Drag it into position and pull a corner to resize it, so it matches the size of the real stamp. Stamps often overlap a signature a little, as they do on paper.

Download the PDF and the stamp is part of it.

## Sign and stamp together

Add your signature the same way, drawn or from a photo, and place it next to the stamp. The editor remembers the last three you used, in your browser only, so next time both are one click away.

## A word of care

A stamp on paper has legal weight because only you hold it. A picture of it can be copied by anyone who has the file or the photo. Keep the photo private, and don’t leave it on a shared computer.

That’s also why the photo is handled in your browser and never uploaded. Your stamp doesn’t end up on someone else’s server.
